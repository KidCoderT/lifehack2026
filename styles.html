Pick a tone. The mechanics are already decided.

All three directions share the same structure: the tree owns the top of Home, the community garden is an isometric plot where tree height is the ranking, and members over baseline turn their tile amber. They differ only in how the app feels to hold. Every number below is real seed data.

Direction A

Field Notes

Signature: the entire interface is monospace. An app about measurement, set in the typeface of measurement. Flat fills, no gradients, no drop shadows — the plot and the numbers do all the work.

Risk: all-mono at consumer scale reads as precise, or as a terminal. The 13px body and open line-height are what decide which.

Direction B

Watt Arcade

Signature: the meter disc. The spinning disc from a real kWh meter sits at the top of Home — fast when you are drawing power, slow when you are saving — with an odometer minting points beneath it. Everything pressable has a 4px underside that collapses.

Risk: skeuomorphic meter plus hard-shadow buttons is loud. It either delights or reads as a toy.

Everything you can press has an underside

Hold the button. The 4px slab collapses and the label drops with it — the same physical feedback Duolingo uses to make a tap feel like an event rather than a state change.

Direction C

Greenhouse

Signature: the sky. The gradient behind your tree tracks the real time of day, and hazes over when you are above baseline. Feedback with no number attached — and a reason the app looks different at 7am and at 11pm.

Risk: no celebration UI at all. Delight has to come from noticing your tree changed, which is slower and quieter than a burst.

Time of day sets the gradient. Being over baseline overrides it with haze — you can tell you had a bad day before you read a single figure.
